What Supplement Brands Don't Tell You About Their Return Policies

A money-back guarantee is one of the first things buyers look for on a supplement website. It sounds like a promise: if the product does not work for you, you get your money back. The reality is usually more complicated, and the details live in the fine print that almost nobody reads.

The first thing to check is the time limit. A "60-day guarantee" may start from the date of purchase, the date of delivery, or the date the order was shipped — and the difference can be a week or more. Some guarantees run for 60 days from purchase but require the return to arrive within that window, which means you may need to post the product back after only a few weeks of use. Read the start date carefully before you rely on the headline number.

The second thing to check is what "return" actually means. Some brands refund the full price, including postage. Others refund the product price only, and deduct the original shipping cost. Some require you to return the empty bottles; others accept a photo of the empty bottle; a few demand the unopened product back, which makes the guarantee useless once you have tried it. And some guarantees apply only to the first bottle, even when you bought six.

The third thing to check is how the refund is processed. A reputable brand tells you plainly: email this address, include your order number, receive a return authorisation, post the bottles, and the refund lands within a stated number of days. A vague guarantee — "contact us and we will make it right" — is not a policy at all. It is a promise to decide later, and the decision may not go your way.

There is also the question of who pays for return postage. International buyers often discover that returning a supplement costs more than the refund is worth, especially when the brand is overseas and the return must be tracked. A guarantee that looks generous on paper can be worthless in practice if the return costs half the product price.

None of this means guarantees are meaningless. A clear, written, generous return policy is a genuine sign of confidence — a brand that expects most buyers to keep the product can afford to refund the few who do not.
